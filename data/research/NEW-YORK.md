## 0. Headline findings (read these first)

1. **SUNY GE changes for Fall 2026.** The Fall-2023 framework (Res. 2021-48) has been amended by **Resolution 2024-64 (Dec 17, 2024)**. It now has 3 core competencies instead of 2. **Civic Discourse** is new and Information Literacy is revised. Both versions have the same 10 knowledge areas, the same 4 mandatory areas and the same 30 credits.
2. **Both systems have a systemwide exam-credit floor, and they differ.**
   - **SUNY policy 1300 (1976):** an AP score of **3+** and the CLEP *subject*-exam "C" score are guaranteed, and at least 30 exam credits must be applicable at any SUNY campus. **"no transfer guarantees will be made"** for CLEP *general* exams. Campuses still decide which requirement the credit fills.
   - **CUNY Policy 1.21 (Credit for Prior Learning):** campuses "**shall award credit**" for AP **3+** and CLEP **50+**. It also says "Institutions may not award credit for scores below those listed." Each college decides whether the credit is a specific course, a Pathways area or an elective.
3. **No SUNY or CUNY four-year campus I checked refuses CLEP outright.** However, **three of them refuse CLEP *general* exams**, which include **CLEP Humanities** and (at Binghamton, explicitly) composition:
   - **Stony Brook:** "The University awards credit for the CLEP … subject examinations only. Credit is not awarded for the CLEP general examinations."
   - **Binghamton (Harpur):** "Credit is only awarded for subject exams, not for general exams. General exams, such as 'Humanities'…" and "Harpur College does not grant composition credit on an exam basis; therefore a student cannot obtain writing credit for a CLEP exam."
   - **Hunter (2019 sheet, possibly superseded by CUNY 1.21):** "Hunter College does NOT accept CLEP GENERAL Examinations."
4. **An AP score of 3 is usually only elective credit at the big campuses.** This is true at UB (`APC999TR`), Baruch (`LAS-7000`, explicitly since Fall 2018), Hunter ("ELECTIVE") and often at Stony Brook ("none" equivalent, though some rows still list an SBC area). A 4 or 5 is usually needed for a specific course.
5. **Tuition is frozen.**
   - SUNY state-operated resident undergraduate: **$295/credit, $3,535/semester, $7,070/year**, for 2026-27.
   - CUNY senior colleges: **$305/credit part-time, $3,465/semester full-time**.
   - CUNY community colleges: **$210/credit, $2,400/semester**.
   - SUNY community colleges vary by college; for example SUNY Niagara charges **$227/credit** for Fall 2026.

## 1. SUNY General Education Framework (SUNY GE)

**Source (A):** https://system.suny.edu/academic-affairs/academic-policies/general-education/suny-ge/ — read 2026-10-01

### Effective dates

- > "On December 17, 2024, the SUNY Board of Trustees passed Resolution 2024-64, which amended Resolution 2021-48, passed by the SUNY Board of Trustees on November 9, 2021, that established the SUNY General Education Framework (SUNY GE)."
- > "The amended SUNY GE is applicable to new first-time students entering all associate and baccalaureate degree programs in or after fall 2026. The SUNY GE adopted in 2021 remains applicable to new first-time students who entered A.A., A.S., and all baccalaureate degree programs in or after fall 2023 but prior to fall 2026 …"
- **Confirmed:** the 2021 framework applies to students entering Fall 2023 through before Fall 2026, and the amended framework applies from Fall 2026. **Grade A.**

### Structure (current, Fall 2026+)

> "The SUNY GE framework includes ***13***(*changed from 12*) categories … ten knowledge and skills areas … and ***three***(*changed from two*) core competencies"

**Knowledge and Skills Areas:** "a minimum of 7 of 10 categories of knowledge and skills are required for AA-, AS-, and all baccalaureate-degree programs".

**Mandatory (4):** "The following four are specifically required for all undergraduate-degree programs"
- Communication – Written and Oral
- Diversity: Equity, Inclusion, and Social Justice
- Mathematics (and Quantitative Reasoning)
- Natural Sciences (and Scientific Reasoning)

**At least 3 of these 6:**
- Humanities
- Social Sciences
- The Arts
- US History and Civic Engagement
- World History and Global Awareness
- World Languages

**Core Competencies (required for all):**
- Critical Thinking and Reasoning
- Information Literacy "(Revised, Effective Fall 2026)"
- Civic Discourse "(New, Effective Fall 2026)"

**Inference (A-sourced):** the Fall 2023 to pre-Fall 2026 version had the same 10 areas and the same 4 mandatory areas. It had **2** competencies, Critical Thinking and Reasoning and Information Literacy, because the page marks Civic Discourse as new and the count as "changed from two". I did not open the separate "Pre-2026 SUNY GE page" the text refers to. **Grade A for the change, B for the exact pre-2026 wording.**

- **Grade A.**

### Exam-credit restrictions inside SUNY GE (important for AP/CLEP)

- Communication guidance: > "Remedial or ESL courses are not acceptable within this category; nor shall students place out of this SUNY GE requirement by high scores on Regents and/or SAT (or similar) exams."
- Mathematics guidance: > "Remedial courses are not acceptable within this category; nor shall students place out of this SUNY GE requirement by high scores on Regents and/or SAT (or similar) exams."
- These rules forbid *placing out*. They do not forbid AP/CLEP *credit* counting. Whether exam credit fills a SUNY GE area is a campus decision; see § 3. **Grade A (quote).**

### Transfer of GE (from the FAQ on the same page)

> "Courses successfully completed with a grade of C or better are guaranteed to transfer. If the course was successfully completed under the previous GE format, the category must be considered as met."

**Grade A.**

## 2. CUNY Pathways Common Core

**Sources (A):**
- https://www.cuny.edu/about/administration/offices/undergraduate-studies/pathways/gened/
- https://www.cuny.edu/about/administration/offices/undergraduate-studies/pathways/gened/flex-commoncore/

Both read 2026-10-01.

### Scope

> "Pathways provides a set of General Education Requirements that every student, starting in Fall 2013, must complete to earn an associate in arts (AA), associate in science (AS) or bachelor's degree from CUNY. (Students pursuing AAS degrees may not have to fulfill all 30 credits of the Common Core.) Additionally, students in bachelor's degree programs must meet the College Option requirement."

### Required Common Core: "(12 credits / 4 courses)"

- English Composition (2 courses)
- Mathematical and Quantitative Reasoning (1 course)
- Life and Physical Sciences (1 course)

### Flexible Common Core: "(18 credits / 6 courses)"

Each area is "3 credits" according to the flex-commoncore page.

- World Cultures and Global Issues (1 course)
- U.S. Experience in Its Diversity (1 course)
- Creative Expression (1 course)
- Individual and Society (1 course)
- Scientific World (1 course)
- "One additional course will be taken from one of the above areas."

**Total:** 12 + 18 = **30 credits.** **Grade A.**

### Rules

- Flex page: > "students in AA, AS and bachelor's degree programs must complete at least one course in each of the five Flexible Core areas and an additional sixth course in one of them. Students can complete no more than two courses from any one discipline or interdisciplinary field.\* In many cases, colleges will specify how the sixth course must be chosen."
- > "\*At Baruch College, students may complete no more than one course in any one discipline or interdisciplinary field."
- Flex page: > "Some math and science courses that satisfy major requirements and Required Core requirements are more than 3 credits, although only 3 credits will count toward the Common Core requirement."

**Grade A.**

### College Option (bachelor's only)

> "Students in bachelor's degree programs are required to take 6 to 12 additional credits of general education that are defined by each college."

Credits-transfer page (see § 6): Associate degree → 6, more than 30 credits → 9, 30 or fewer credits → 12. **Grade A.**

## 3. Credit by exam (AP, CLEP, IB)

### 3a. SUNY systemwide: Policy 1300 "Award of Academic Credit by Evaluation"

**Source (A):** https://www.suny.edu/sunypp/documents.cfm?doc_id=163 — effective July 23, 1976; applies to Community Colleges and State-Operated Campuses.

- > "Published Examinations - For purposes of providing minimum university-wide guarantees to all University students, students may apply at least 30 credit hours earned through published subject examinations, including those offered as transfer credit, toward fulfillment of degree requirements at any University campus where they are enrolled."
- > "a. College-Level Examination Program (Subject Examinations) - Mean score obtained by persons from the standardization group who had earned a grade of C in a formal course; … c. Advanced Placement Program - A score of 3 or higher within the scale of 5 points used for this program."
- > "A campus may award credit for the general examinations of the College-Level Examination Program (CLEP), but no transfer guarantees will be made to students for these examinations."
- > "Campuses will not be expected to count credit hours toward fulfillment of degree requirements in areas that would normally not receive credit during an evaluation of transfer credits."
- Residence: > "Credit hours earned through a demonstration of proficiency, either through published examinations or through examinations constructed and graded by a campus, may not be counted toward satisfaction of any existing residence requirements."

**Reading:**
- SUNY guarantees that AP 3+ and CLEP-subject credit at the C-level score *count as credit*, with a floor of 30 such credits.
- It does **not** fix which course or GE area the credit fills.
- The policy names no numeric CLEP score; "mean score of C-grade group" is the old norm-referenced definition. Today CLEP's recommended C-level score is **50**, per Brooklyn's page: "the recommended credit-granting score for all exams is 50, a score representative of a grade of C".
- Which CLEP titles count as "general examinations" is not defined in the policy. Campus pages give **Humanities** as the example (Binghamton). Others historically in that group, such as College Composition, Social Sciences & History, Natural Sciences and College Mathematics, are **RECALLED — not verified** as "general" in the policy's sense.

**Grade A (text). Interpretation of "general exams" list: C.**

IB: no SUNY-wide IB rule found in policy 1300. UB's page applies an IB diploma 30+ → 30 credits rule, which matches CUNY's. **Not found as SUNY-wide policy.**

### 3b. CUNY university-wide: Policy 1.21 Credit for Prior Learning, plus BoT Policy 1.071 (AP) and 1.192 (IB)

**Sources (A):**
- https://www.cuny.edu/academics/academic-policy/credit-prior-learning/ (full policy text on page)
- https://www.cuny.edu/admissions/undergraduate/apply/credit/

The PDF at policy.cuny.edu was unreachable; its search snippet matches the page text.

- > "If a student achieves the score listed on an AP, AICE, IB, DSST, DLPT, or CLEP exam, CUNY institutions shall award credit even if they do not offer the subject area."
- > "3. Institutions may not award credit for scores below those listed."
- AP: > "The CUNY Board of Trustees adopted a policy in 2017, that all undergraduate colleges and schools at the University shall grant course credit for advanced placement courses offered in secondary schools provided the student scores 3 or above on the advanced placement test. (BTM Policy 1.071 Advanced Placement)"
- CLEP: > "Credits shall be awarded for minimum score of 50."
- IB: > "all undergraduate colleges at the University will award 30 credits to students who have completed an International Baccalaureate (IB) diploma with a score of 30 or higher. Students who have completed an IB diploma with a score of 29 or less and students who did not complete a diploma will be guaranteed credit for higher-level IB exams with scores of '5' or better." (BTM Policy 1.192)
- Applicability limit: > "5. The knowledge and skills being assessed shall fulfill degree requirements. If the assessment does not apply to unfulfilled general education, major requirements or electives, the college does not have to accept the credits toward the degree."
- Residency: > "Since PLA Credit is viewed as a form of transfer credit, it shall not be utilized to fulfill institutional residency requirements."
- FAQ: > "Since CPL credits are considered a form of transfer credits, they will be combined with credit-bearing transfer courses up to the maximum number of allowable transfer credits."
- FAQ: > "Prior learning credits will be entered onto the student transcript as a course equivalent, as a General Education curriculum area equivalent, or as elective credit."
- Pathways page (credits-transfer): > "CPL credits may fulfill Common Core requirements as designated by colleges. … If a college allows a AP credit to count toward a Common Core requirement, a score of 3 or higher is required."

**Grade A.**

**Reading:** CUNY colleges must award credit for AP 3+ and CLEP 50+. **Which Pathways area the credit fills is each college's decision.** The CLEP general/subject distinction does not appear in CUNY 1.21, which lists the exam program, not individual titles.

## 6. Transfer guarantees

### SUNY

**SUNY Policy 1007 Transfer and Articulation** (https://www.suny.edu/sunypp/documents.cfm?doc_id=37, effective Nov 19, 1990). **Grade A.**

- > "A. New York State residents who are graduates of a State University of New York (University) two-year college, including the community colleges operating within the program of the University, and who possess an A.A. or A.S. degree, shall be guaranteed an opportunity to continue their education on a full-time basis at a baccalaureate campus of the University."
- > "1. Graduates of two-year colleges within the University with an A.A. or A.S. degree, when accepted in a parallel program at a baccalaureate campus of the University, shall be accorded full junior status and be given the opportunity to complete the requirements for a bachelor's degree within four additional semesters of full-time work."
- > "2. … shall be granted full credit for general education courses taken and not be required to repeat successfully completed courses with similar curricular content."
- > "B. New York State residents who are graduates of a City University two-year college, and who possess an A.A. or A.S. degree, shall be guaranteed the same opportunity …"

**SUNY Transfer Policies page** (https://www.suny.edu/attend/get-started/transfer-students/suny-transfer-policies/). **Grade A.**

- > "Guaranteed admission to a SUNY four-year campus for New York students transferring directly from a SUNY campus with an AA or AS degree."
- > "Guaranteed junior standing for SUNY students who graduate with an AA or AS degree and transfer to a parallel program at a SUNY four-year campus."
- > "Satisfactorily completed general education requirements at one SUNY campus will meet the same requirement at another SUNY campus."

**SUNY Seamless Transfer** (https://transfer.suny.edu/students/seamless-transfer/). **Grade A.**

- > "Because SUNY has this common framework, A.A. and A.S. graduates are guaranteed credit transfer for general education."
- > "When you satisfactorily complete a general education course on a SUNY campus, the campus will provide a General Education Transcript Addendum (GETA) … Any SUNY campus receiving your transcript and credit, is required to accept the course and show that the general education requirement is also complete. (2013 Seamless Transfer Policy)"
- > "All approved and listed SUNY Transfer Path courses are guaranteed to transfer as courses in the major or required cognates - not just as electives - if completed with the grade of C or better."
- > "Local general education requirements beyond the SUNY-wide requirements cannot require you to exceed the number of credits to graduation required of first year starting students in the same program."

**Caveat worth showing a student:** the guarantee is admission to **a** SUNY four-year campus ("a baccalaureate campus"), **not to the campus or program of choice**. Junior status applies "when accepted in a parallel program".

### CUNY

**CUNY credits-transfer page** (https://www.cuny.edu/about/administration/offices/undergraduate-studies/pathways/credits-tranfer/). **Grade A.**

- > "Students who have earned a bachelor's, associate in arts, or associate in sciences degree from a regionally accredited and recognized institution of higher education and who transfer to another CUNY college will be deemed to have automatically fulfilled the Common Core requirements for a CUNY degree."
- > "If a student fulfills a Common Core requirement at one CUNY college and transfers to another CUNY college, the requirement will count as fulfilled."
- > "Courses passed for credit at any CUNY college will transfer for credit at any other CUNY college."
- College Option after transfer: "Earned Associate Degree — 6 College Option credits required; Earned More Than 30 Credits — 9 …; Earned 30 or Fewer Credits — 12".
- Note that the AA/AS rule covers **any regionally accredited** institution, not only CUNY.

**SUNY → CUNY (Baruch's own page, A):**
> "Effective Fall 2018 SUNY transfer students who have completed a bachelor's, AA or AS degree or the entire 30-credit GER from SUNY will be deemed to have completed CUNY Pathways Common Core requirement." — https://enrollmentmanagement.baruch.cuny.edu/undergraduate-admissions/transfer-policies/

Whether this SUNY→CUNY rule is CUNY-wide or Baruch-only was **not verified**.

# What actually travels on Reels right now — and what I could not verify

Researched 2026-09-19. Sources at the bottom; every claim here links to one.

## Read this first: what I could not find

**I could not get per-post metrics for specific viral posts from the last
month.** Instagram does not publish them, the weekly trend roundups that do
exist describe *formats and audio* rather than individual posts, and I cannot
browse Instagram from this environment. So there is no "this Reel got 4.2M
views" table below, because I would have had to make it up.

The hard numbers I *did* find are trend-level, not post-level: a 1979 disco
track driving a **+277% streaming jump** as its Reels trend spread, and JENNIE's
"Less Than a Lover" used in **over 221K Reels** since July. Neither is your
niche and neither is actionable for you.

What I *can* give you, sourced, is the mechanism — what Instagram has publicly
confirmed it ranks on, what formats are working in your two niches, and one
directly relevant precedent. That is more useful than a leaderboard anyway.

## 1. The three signals, and what they mean mechanically

Mosseri has named the same three ranking signals through 2026: **watch time,
sends per reach, and likes per reach.**

**Watch time is #1, and it counts replays.** Instagram measures total seconds
watched including repeat views rather than counting 3-second views — so *a 15s
Reel watched three times outranks a 60s Reel watched once.*

> **What this changes for you:** my first draft specced 25–30s A-slots. That was
> wrong. **Cut them to 12–18s** and make the last frame flow into the first so a
> replay feels deliberate rather than accidental. Both files are corrected.

**Sends per reach is the strongest signal for reaching people who don't follow
you** — reportedly worth roughly 3–5× a like for unconnected reach. Likes skew
toward reach among your existing followers; sends are how you escape them.

> **What this changes for you:** "tag a friend" is the wrong ask. A tag is a
> comment. Ask for the **send** — "DM this to someone applying next year". You
> have ~0 followers, so essentially *all* of your reach has to come from sends.
> This is not one lever among several. For you it is the lever.

**Hashtags are capped at 5 and do not drive reach.** Instagram cut the limit
from 30 to 5 in December 2025, and Mosseri's position is blunt: hashtags help
search, they are "just not an important thing for reach."

> **What this changes for you:** my draft had 10-tag blocks. They would have been
> rejected or truncated, and they were never going to help. Now 5, chosen for
> topical clarity. Corrected in both files.
>
> This one is worth noticing for its own sake: I gave you advice that was true
> in 2024 and is false now. It is *exactly* the failure mode the app exists to
> prevent — a claim that is true somewhere else, or true at some other time,
> repeated with confidence. I caught it by checking rather than by remembering.

## 2. What's working in your two niches

### Build-in-public / devlog

The format that works is **serialized, not viral-per-post**: the same visual
setup, the same caption pattern, the same numbering, with **one variable
changing** each time. Reels strategy in 2026 has moved from chasing isolated
viral ideas toward repeatable structures an audience recognises in half a
second.

**Your series is already built this way** — identical cover template, `R01…R33`,
`Day N · Sept X`, one bug or one fact changing. That was the right call and you
should lean on it harder, not loosen it.

Two more consistent findings: **raw phone-shot beats high production** for this
audience, and one recording session should fan out into several clips plus a
longer cut — which is exactly the batching schedule in `BUILD-IN-PUBLIC.md`.

### College cost / CLEP / dual enrollment

The demand is real and the content is actively circulating. The claims doing
the rounds are things like *four dual-enrolment classes in high school ≈ $10,000
off*, and **Modern States giving free CLEP study material and free exam
vouchers** — that last one is already a row in your dataset.

**Your unfair advantage here is that most of this content is directionally true
and locally wrong** — which is the exact thing that cost your friend $285. You
are not competing on "here's a college hack". You are competing on *"that hack
is false at your campus, and here's the statute."* Nobody else in the niche can
make that post, because nobody else has the dataset.

## 3. The one precedent that actually matters

Rudrank Riyam won the **Shipaton Build in Public Award** with daily video logs
and regular posting throughout the event. The line worth pinning above your desk
is his own account of how he knew he'd won:

> **By day 40, he felt confident he would win — because his competition had
> given up.** He didn't slow down on posting.

He also said flatly that without Shipaton and #BuildInPublic, his app *"would
never have made it to the App Store."*

**Your real opponent is attrition, not other people's edit quality.** Most
people who start a daily series stop around day 4. Eleven days of showing up
beats eleven days of trying to go viral, and it is the thing this particular
award is actually looking at.

## 4. The uncomfortable part: virality is not the prize

You asked how to go viral. With 11 days left, I think chasing that is the wrong
objective, and I'd rather say so than optimise for the thing you asked for.

**The Build in Public award is judged on the journey you shared — not on view
counts.** A Reel with 400 views that shows a real bug being found and fixed does
more for that entry than 40,000 views on a trending-audio skit. And the trends
that are actually moving right now — the disco "abundance frequency" trend,
"whimsymaxxing", "Two Best Friends" — have nothing to do with your build. Hopping
on them buys views that a judge will correctly read as noise.

There is also a brand cost specific to *you*. This product's entire argument is
that overclaiming costs students money. The standard virality playbook — round
the number up, drop the caveat, lead with the most extreme case — is the thing
you built the app to refuse. **You cannot run that playbook and keep the pitch.**

So: optimise for **sends and completion**, which are honest and which the
algorithm genuinely rewards, and let reach be a by-product. If something breaks
out, good. If nothing does, you still have the entry that wins the category.

## 5. What to change, concretely

Already applied to `BUILD-IN-PUBLIC.md` and `REEL-CAPTIONS.md`:

- [x] A-slots cut from 25–30s to **12–18s** — replays are counted watch time
- [x] **Loop the last frame into the first** so replays look intentional
- [x] Hashtags **10 → 5**, framed as search not reach
- [x] "Tag someone" → **"send this to someone"** in the CTA
- [x] **Mirror every cut to YouTube Shorts and TikTok** — one algorithm is a
      single point of failure, and the edit is already done

Still yours to do:

- [ ] **Hook in frame 1, not second 2.** The cover card is already text-first;
      make sure the *video* is too. No fade-in, no logo, no "so basically".
- [ ] **Caption the first three words on screen, large.** Sound-off is the
      default; the hook has to land silently.
- [ ] **Reply to every comment in the first hour.** Comment velocity feeds
      Explore, and at your follower count each reply is a meaningful fraction of
      total engagement.
- [ ] **Post R19 twice** — once on day 7 as scheduled, once on day 11 as the
      series recap's opener. It is the most distinctive thing you have and a
      week-old post reaches a different slice of people.

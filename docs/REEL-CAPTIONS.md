# A-slot caption variants (A/B)

Two captions per A-slot Reel, built on **different strategies rather than
different wording**. If the two variants say the same thing in different words,
the test tells you nothing.

- **Variant A — the receipt.** Leads with the fact, the statute, the number. Low
  emotion, high save-and-share value. Reads like something you'd forward to a
  friend who's applying.
- **Variant B — the person.** Leads with who it happened to and what it cost
  them. Higher comment rate, lower save rate.

## How to run the test honestly

**Do not judge these on views.** A caption barely touches initial reach — the
first two seconds of video do that. What a caption moves is **shares, saves and
comments**, so those are the only numbers worth comparing.

Rules that keep the test meaningful:

1. **One variable.** Same video, same cover, same posting time-of-day, same
   hashtag set. If you change the hook card too, you've learned nothing.
2. **Alternate, don't split.** You have eleven A-slots, not an audience big
   enough to split. Run A on odd-numbered Reels (R01, R07, R13, R19, R25, R31)
   and B on even (R04, R10, R16, R22, R28), then compare the two groups.
3. **Four posts minimum before you conclude anything.** One Reel outperforming
   is noise. Three in a row in the same direction is a signal.
4. **Write down the numbers on day 6.** Mid-series is when a switch is still
   worth making. On day 10 it isn't.

**The first line is the whole caption.** Instagram truncates around 125
characters behind "… more", and most people never tap it. Every first line
below stands alone and never repeats the on-screen hook — that text is already
on their screen, and repeating it wastes the one line you get.

**Every caption ends with the standard footer:**

```
Open source, MIT: github.com/feedddosever/whatsgoingon
Built for @revenuecat Shipaton 2026 🐱
```

## R01 · Day 1 · "3 exams. $285. Worth $0."

**A — the receipt**

> The University of California awards no CLEP credit. Not reduced credit. Not elective credit. None.
>
> That's not a rule anyone hides. It's just that CLEP advice is written nationally and enforced locally, so "widely accepted" is true — somewhere else.
>
> I'm building the thing that tells you which sentence applies to *you*, before you pay the exam fee.
>
> Day 1 of 11. What's the worst college-cost advice you were given?

**B — the person**

> My friend sat three CLEP exams on the strength of a blog post. They were going to a UC.
>
> Nobody lied to them. The post was accurate — for a Cal State, for a private, for half of Florida. Not for where they were going.
>
> $285 and a summer, for credit that doesn't exist at their campus.
>
> So I'm building the thing that would have told them in ten seconds. Day 1 of 11.

## R04 · Day 2 · "$2,505 → $1,002"

**A — the receipt**

> Florida publishes one statewide table and all 12 state universities are legally required to follow it.
>
> Rule 6A-10.024. Same three CLEP exams that clear nothing in California clear 3 of the 5 general-education requirements there.
>
> Same student, same scores, same week. The state is the variable.
>
> Which state should I map next?

**B — the person**

> Same student. Same three exams. I just changed which state she was applying to.
>
> California: nothing. She still owes every requirement.
> Florida: three of five, cleared, by rule.
>
> What she still has to pay for drops from $2,505 to $1,002 — and she did nothing differently.
>
> This is the part nobody tells 17-year-olds.

## R07 · Day 3 · "Finish it once. Anywhere. Statute."

**A — the receipt**

> Texas Education Code §61.822: finish the 42-hour core at any Texas public college and the receiving university **must** substitute it for its own.
>
> Not "usually accepts". Not "subject to review". Must. And you may not be required to take additional core courses.
>
> It's the single most valuable fact for a Texas student, and no university website tells you — because it isn't theirs to give.
>
> Texans: did anyone ever tell you this?

**B — the person**

> If you're in Texas and about to retake a general-ed class you already passed somewhere else, stop.
>
> There's a line in the state education code that says the university has to take the whole 42-hour core as a block. Not negotiate it. Take it.
>
> I built an app that surfaces this above everything else, because every student I asked had no idea it existed.
>
> Tag a Texas student who needs this.

## R10 · Day 4 · "102,000 → 260,000 in one year"

**A — the receipt**

> Texas HB 8 created FAST — Financial Aid for Swift Transfer. If you're eligible for free or reduced-price lunch, dual-credit college courses cost you nothing.
>
> First year it ran, enrolment of economically disadvantaged students in dual credit more than doubled. 102,000 → 260,000.
>
> The programme works. The problem is finding out about it while you're still in high school.
>
> Source in the app, on the row.

**B — the person**

> The cheapest college credit in America is free, and most of the kids who qualify will never hear about it.
>
> Texas: if you're on free or reduced lunch, dual credit costs $0 under a programme called FAST.
>
> Not a discount. Not a loan. Zero.
>
> When it launched, the number of low-income students taking dual credit went from 102,000 to 260,000 in a single year — which tells you exactly how many were priced out before.
>
> Who needs to see this?

## R13 · Day 5 · "Accepted ≠ useful"

**A — the receipt**

> A Cal State will accept your CLEP credit. Up to 30 units, toward your degree.
>
> It cannot satisfy a single Cal-GETC general-education requirement.
>
> So the credit posts, your transcript looks fine, and your requirement list hasn't moved. Two different failures, and the second is far easier to miss than a flat refusal.
>
> "Accepted" and "useful" are different words for a reason.

**B — the person**

> The worst version of this isn't the campus that says no. It's the one that says yes.
>
> Your credit posts. Your transcript looks completely normal. And you still owe the requirement you thought you'd just cleared.
>
> You find out in your final year, when the degree audit runs.
>
> I built the warning for exactly this. It's amber, not red, because nothing is actually broken — that's the problem.

## R16 · Day 6 · "1C is CSU only"

**A — the receipt**

> Cal-GETC Area 1C — oral communication — is a CSU requirement. UC doesn't require it.
>
> I was imposing it on UC-bound students anyway: three units they never owed, $1,725 at UC Berkeley's rate.
>
> Fixed by making every requirement name the systems that require it. That same field is what later let the app cover three states instead of one.
>
> $19,550 → $17,825, from reading the standard properly.

**B — the person**

> I spent a week telling UC students to take a class they don't have to take.
>
> Not maliciously — I'd read one requirement pattern and assumed it applied to both systems. It doesn't. Area 1C is Cal State only.
>
> Three units. $1,725. Invented by me, out of an assumption.
>
> This is why every row in this app has to cite where it came from. My memory is not a source.

## R19 · Day 7 · "The lowest-risk route came back empty" ⭐ PIN THIS

**A — the receipt**

> This is my app declining to answer.
>
> In Texas I don't have exam mappings confirmed against each campus's published policy — Texas sets a statutory floor on the *score* but publishes no statewide table of which exam clears what.
>
> So the lowest-risk route returns nothing, and says so: "there is nothing here we would stake your money on."
>
> I could have made Texas look as strong as California by relaxing one confidence level. The whole product would have been worth less.

**B — the person**

> The hardest thing I've shipped is a screen that says "I don't know."
>
> Every instinct says fill it. Show something. A confident guess converts better than an empty state, always.
>
> But a student who acts on a wrong transfer-credit claim loses real money and a real semester. So the app is allowed to refuse.
>
> If you're building where being wrong is expensive: make "I don't know" a real state in your data model, not a fallback in your UI.

## Reusing these for B and C slots

Don't. B-slot (build log) and C-slot (the why) captions are in
`docs/BUILD-IN-PUBLIC.md` and are deliberately shorter — a build-log Reel's job
is to show the diff, and a long caption competes with it. The A-slot is the only
one where the caption is doing real work, which is why it's the only one tested.

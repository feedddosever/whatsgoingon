# What "how do I save on college" can actually end in

Five routes, and they are not variations on each other — they have different
prices, different people they suit, and different ways of failing. Written from
`data/research/MASTER-LIST.md` §§ 8, 9, 12.

Figures are whole-degree ranges for a resident, not the app's per-requirement
numbers. **Every one of them assumes the destination accepts what you bought,
which is the entire question this app exists to answer.**

---

## 1 · Dual enrolment while still at school

**≈ $0.** The cheapest credit in America, and the one with the hardest deadline:
you have to still be in high school.

**50 of 51 states have a programme.** Florida exempts dual-enrolment students
from tuition and fees by statute (§ 1007.271). Texas FAST makes it free for
students on free or reduced lunch — enrolment of economically disadvantaged
students more than doubled in its first year. Ohio's College Credit Plus,
Washington's Running Start, Minnesota's PSEO, Idaho's Advanced Opportunities
(which also pays AP and CLEP fees) are all free or near it.

**How it fails:** nobody tells a 15-year-old in time. It is the largest saving
in this document and it expires silently.

## 2 · Exam credit — AP, CLEP, IB, A Level, DSST

**$0–$600 for a year of credit.** Modern States gives a free course and a
voucher covering the **$97** CLEP fee; DSST is free for serving members at a
DANTES site; many schools pay AP fees outright.

**How it fails, and this is the app's core case:** the same exam is worth wildly
different amounts by destination. CLEP clears general education in Florida **by
rule** and cannot satisfy Cal-GETC **at all**; the University of California
awards nothing for CLEP or DSST while accepting AP, IB and A Level. Florida
awards credit for many exams whose courses are **not** part of the core — credit
that counts toward the degree and clears no requirement.

Check the destination before you book the exam. Not after.

## 3 · Community college first, then transfer

**≈ $1,500–6,000 for two years**, often $0 with aid. California's fee is
**$46/unit**, unchanged since 2012, and the College Promise Grant waives it
outright.

The leverage is not the tuition — it is the **statewide guarantee**. Finish the
Texas Core and the receiving university *must* substitute it. A Florida AA
guarantees admission to a state university with junior standing. **46 of 51
states have an instrument of some kind**, and no single campus page will tell
you, because it is not any one campus's to give.

**How it fails:** the guarantee has conditions that are prose and not arithmetic
— Arizona's AGEC needs a 2.5, Michigan's MTA a 2.0 in every course,
MassTransfer a 2.0 overall. And **Oregon and Washington count in quarter
credits**, so "30 credits" is not what a semester student thinks it is.

## 4 · Third-party providers — Sophia, Study.com, Saylor, StraighterLine

**≈ $500–2,000 for a year of credit**, and the highest-variance route here.
Sophia is $99/month with no course cap; Saylor is free plus $5 to sit each
exam; ASU Universal Learner is $25 up front and $400 **only if you pass**.

**This is not a state route, it is a destination route.** The credit lands on
the *provider's* transcript, so the receiving college is deciding whether to
accept a company's paperwork. Eight public destinations appear on three or more
provider partner lists and are the realistic targets: **Charter Oak (CT),
Thomas Edison (NJ), SUNY Empire (NY), UMGC (MD), UMPI (ME), CSU Global (CO),
Purdue Global (IN), UAGC (AZ)** — all online.

**How it fails:** partner listings are *programme-scoped*, caps apply (Charter
Oak 90/45; CSU Long Beach 20%), third-party credit is ungraded so it cannot lift
a GPA toward a guarantee, and **nothing found lets third-party credit into a
statewide general-education block by right**. Florida is the strongest case in
the country and all it gives you is the right to be *evaluated*, on request,
before your first term.

## 5 · Somebody else pays

**$0 to you.** The route nobody models because it is not academic.

Employer tuition benefits are the largest single line: Amazon Career Choice and
Walmart's Live Better U cover tuition from early in employment; Starbucks funds
a first bachelor's through ASU Online; UPS runs $5,250/year to a $25,000
lifetime cap. **IRS § 127 excludes $5,250 a year from income**, which is why so
many programmes stop exactly there.

State aid is the other half: **39 states have a named programme** in this
dataset. But only **12 of them are a need-tested waiver or a genuinely universal
promise** — the two kinds this app is willing to subtract from a price. The
other 27 are gated on age, graduation year, field of study or service, and the
app shows them without applying them, because quoting a discount a student may
not get is wrong in the direction nobody reports.

## Which one is yours

| If you are… | Start with |
|---|---|
| In school, any year | **1**, then 2. Nothing else comes close, and the door shuts at graduation. |
| Out of school, aiming at a named public campus | **3**, checked against your state's guarantee, with **2** filling what it allows |
| Out of school, aiming at *a* degree rather than a campus | **4**, targeting one of the eight destinations above |
| Employed by a large company | **5** first. Check before you pay for anything at all. |
| On a gap year, low income | **3** — the waiver states make it nearly free, and the guarantee is the real prize |
| Serving or a veteran | **2** via DSST (free at a DANTES site), then **5** |

## What none of these routes survives

Buying credit before you know what your destination does with it. Every failure
mode above is the same failure: advice that is true somewhere, applied where it
is not. That is the whole reason this app cites a source for every claim and
refuses to guess when it has none.
